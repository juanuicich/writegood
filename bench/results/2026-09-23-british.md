# British English rules and a reworked topic flow

23 September 2026. Rule set `2026-09-23-british`, against the baseline in
`BENCHMARKS.md` ("Baseline for rule changes"): rule set
`2026-09-23-shipped`, results `noise-1` to `-4`.

## What changed in the rules

**British English, all nine rules.** The convention:

- Spelling: British, with -ise (`summarise`). The rules had no other word
  that British spelling changes. `program` stays, because it names a
  computer program. The category values, such as `nominalization`, are
  identifiers and stay as they are.
- Quotation marks: single quotation marks for every quoted example and
  every quote in the prompt body. Double marks would be used only for a
  quotation inside a quotation, and no rule has one. The TOML frontmatter
  keeps double quotation marks, because TOML needs them.
- Punctuation: a full stop or comma goes inside the marks only when it
  belongs to the quoted text. The rules already followed this.
- No serial comma. The rules already followed this.

Meaning, structure, the frontmatter keys and values and what each rule
flags did not change in the eight other rules. So for those rules, the
change from `shipped` is the quotation marks alone.

**Topic flow.** The baseline runs flagged 44 false topic-flow findings
against 5 reference items. Most opened on something the reader could
already place: the narrator, a person or place named earlier in the draft,
a part of something just named, the next event in a sequence, one cost
after another cost, or a contrast. Many were flagged only because the link
to the sentence before came after the subject. Two were passive openings,
which the missing-actor pass reports.

The new rule:

- defines the first kind of break as an opening the reader cannot connect
  to anything before it, and names the clearest case: an aside, where the
  next sentence continues from the sentence before the aside;
- states the test as a question about the reader: at the subject, does the
  reader know how the opening relates to what came before?
- lists seven kinds of opening that are linked and must not be flagged;
- adds two items to 'Do not flag': a linked opening whose link to the
  sentence before comes late, and a passive or impersonal opening;
- redefines the severities to match, and adds 'Most paragraphs have none.'

The rule text is the whole definition. No code changed. Its examples are
new and use none of the corpus's wording.

## How it was run

Four runs, as the baseline was run:

```
bun bench/scripts/run.ts --provider deepseek --model deepseek-flash --thinking off \
  --rules 2026-09-23-british --pipeline hybrid --skip filler-words --label british-ds-N
bun bench/scripts/run.ts --provider jev --thinking off \
  --rules 2026-09-23-british --pipeline hybrid --passes filler-words --label british-jev-N
bun bench/scripts/merge.ts --label british-N \
  --base bench/results/2026-09-23-british-ds-N.json \
  --take bench/results/2026-09-23-british-jev-N.json
```

The fast passes ran on DeepSeek Flash direct, thinking off, with the
verifier. Paragraph order ran at thinking high. Filler words ran on Jev
(`jev-1.13.0`, method `sentence`, keep 0.5).

Overall F1 rose 5.9 points, above the 4.5 points that the baseline puts at
noise. The gain is in precision. Recall did not change.

**Topic flow.** False findings fell from 18, 10, 14 and 2 per run to 2, 1,
1 and 3: from 44 to 7 over four runs. Hits fell from 10 to 7. The new rule
found the item in the story in all four runs, the item in the essay in two
and the item in the memo in one. The baseline found these in four, four and
one runs. In `on-writing.md`, the baseline found one of its two items in one
run, and the new rule found none. F1 rose 10.8 points. Topic flow has 5
reference items, so its F1 alone is not a firm result. The fall in false
findings, from 44 to 7, is much larger than the spread between runs.

**British English.** Without topic flow, the other eight passes scored
74.5% in the baseline and 77.3% here, a gain of 2.8 points. That is under
the noise threshold. So the quotation marks changed nothing that this
benchmark can detect.

Two passes with only the British change moved by more than 10 points:

- Repeated phrasing rose 16.0 points. Its false findings fell from 22 to 7
  over four runs.
- Length fell 12.8 points. It found 9 reference items where the baseline
  found 11, and it made 11 false findings where the baseline made 6.

Length has 6 items and repeated phrasing 11. A pass with 5 or 6 items moved
11 to 45 points between baseline runs with nothing changed. The two moves go in opposite
directions on rules that changed in the same way. This benchmark cannot
tell whether they come from the quotation marks, from DeepSeek, or from
chance. The instruction was to keep the British change, so length keeps
it. A later change to length should be judged on more drafts.

No pass needed its content change reverted. Topic flow improved, and it is
the only rule whose content changed.
