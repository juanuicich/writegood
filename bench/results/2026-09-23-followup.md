# Follow-up tests: Luna, DeepSeek direct, Gemini, Jev Method 2

23 September 2026. Rule set `2026-09-23-rewrite` unless a section says
otherwise. Every OpenRouter call was pinned to the model's own vendor, with
fallbacks off. Each result names the provider that served it. The scores are
for the eight fast passes on the four scored drafts (76 reference items),
unless a table says otherwise.

`scripts/stages.ts` scores a run twice: the candidates before the verifier,
and the findings after it. Several conclusions below use it.

## 1. GPT-6 Luna: why so many empty replies

| Result | Effort | F1 | P | R | Empty pass replies | Recall before verifier | Time per draft | Cost per draft |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| or-luna-none-fast-1 | none | 62.7% | 88.1% | 48.7% | 77% | 77.6% | 9.6 s | $0.0136 |
| or-luna-none-fast-2 | none | 64.3% | 94.9% | 48.7% | 77% | 75.0% | 10.8 s | $0.0137 |
| or-luna-low-fast-1 | low | 67.1% | 67.1% | 67.1% | 59% | 84.2% | 37.0 s | $0.0308 |
| or-luna-low-fast-2 | low | 69.7% | 68.4% | 71.1% | 60% | 81.6% | 28.7 s | $0.0300 |
| or-dsflash-fast-1, -2 (reference) | off | 64.9%, 64.6% | 64%, 60% | 66%, 70% | 40%, 38% | 94.7%, 96.1% | 5.1 s, 5.7 s | $0.0168, $0.0160 |

Effort explains the low recall. The harness does not.

- The harness reads Luna's replies correctly. The empty replies are a literal
  `[]` in the message content. The same code reads DeepSeek's findings on the
  same prompts. No call failed, and no reply was cut off. All 84 verifier
  replies of run 1 parse into keep or drop verdicts, with no stray items.
- At effort none, Luna returns `[]` for 77% of pass calls on the scored
  drafts. DeepSeek returns an empty array for 39%. Luna's candidates reach 76%
  of the reference items; DeepSeek's reach 95%.
- The verifier removes about 28 points of recall for both models: Luna from
  77% to 49%, DeepSeek from 95% to 66%. So the verifier is not the part that
  treats Luna differently. Luna starts with fewer candidates, and the verifier
  then cuts the same share.
- At effort low, Luna returns `[]` for 60% of pass calls. F1 rises 4 to 5
  points, and recall rises 18 to 22 points. Precision falls from 88–95% to
  67–68%. The runs cost 2.2 times as much and take about 3 times as long.
- On `chapter.md`, Luna at none returned `[]` for 781 of 792 pass calls and
  gave 15 candidates. The verifier kept none. DeepSeek gave 441 candidates on
  the same draft and kept 49. The chapter is the app's specification, written
  to the same rules the passes check, so it has few problems: DeepSeek's
  verifier kept 11% of its chapter candidates, against 34% on the scored
  drafts. Its prompts also carry a 16,000-character window, against about
  3,000 characters for a scored draft.

Not separated: whether the long window adds to the empty replies on the
chapter. That needs a chapter run at low, which was not part of this test.

| or-dsflash-fast-2 | `run.ts` | OpenRouter | 64.6% | 60.2% | 69.7% | 5.7 s | $0.0160 |

With all nine passes, ds-direct-hybrid-1 and -2 score 70.4% and 64.5%
(R-1 and R-2: 71.2% and 71.8%). Paragraph order scored 92% in both. Wall per
draft was 40.7 s and 32.9 s, set by paragraph order at thinking high.

OpenRouter does not explain the gap. Through `run.ts`, the direct API scores
a mean of 65.5% and OpenRouter 64.8%.

The gap to X3 is the harness or run-to-run variation. Two runs cannot tell
which. The two direct runs through `run.ts` differ by 6 points, which is as
large as the gap. Run 2 lost precision on topic flow (13 false positives,
against 4 in run 1) and on length. The two harnesses do the same steps: the
same prompts, filters and verifier prompt, three votes, two keeps. Three
differences were found. The scratch harness sent 8 calls at a time per
draft, not 32. It did not apply the paragraph filter to topic flow. It ran
the verifier as a separate stage after all pass calls. None of these is
likely to lower precision in `run.ts`.

| X3-tf-para-1, -2 | off | 70.2%, 70.5% | 71%, 69% | 70%, 72% | 5.9 s, 6.1 s | - | $0.017 | 0 |
| agy, Gemini 3.8 Flash, with verifier | - | 77–79% | - | - | - | - | - | - |

No agy result file is on this branch. The agy row is the figure given in the
brief.

- Gemini at low scores 74% F1, 4 to 9 points above DeepSeek. It is 3 to 5
  points below agy.
- It costs about $0.18 per draft, 11 times DeepSeek. OpenRouter reported no
  cache reads for 468,000 input tokens in a run. DeepSeek read 35% to 40% of
  its input from cache.
- A draft takes 23 to 24 seconds, against 5 to 6 for DeepSeek. Most calls
  answer in 1.5 seconds, but a few pass calls take 10 to 34 seconds, and they
  set the wall.
- Gemini accepts `minimal`. It rejects reasoning off and effort `none` with
  "Reasoning is mandatory". `minimal` used as many reasoning tokens as `low`
  (97,000 against 74,000 and 88,000 in a run), so it saved nothing. It ran
  once. A second run would have passed the key limit. Google returned 429 for
  10 pass calls on one draft in that run, so its recall is low.

Concurrency. The first attempt ran four drafts at once, up to 128 calls in
flight. OpenRouter returned 429 for 43 verifier calls: it limits this model to
300 requests per minute, and four drafts send about 340 calls in 15 seconds.
That run was discarded. The kept runs used `run.ts --serial 60`: one draft at a
time, 32 calls in flight, 60 seconds between drafts. The two runs at low had
no 429. One draft sends 85 to 95 calls, so the app's limit of 32 per draft
works for one draft of this size. A draft the size of `chapter.md` (about 900
calls) would pass 300 per minute.

## 4. Jev with the rule text alone

TypeSafe Jev. Code only segments with `Intl.Segmenter`, lists word spans,
locates quotes and removes overlaps. The pass's rule text decides what is
flagged.

### 4a. Method 2 for filler words and missing actor

`scripts/jev-nolist/run-rule-choice.ts`. Stage 1 is Method 1: one Noul per
sentence, with the rule text as the question. Stage 2 runs for each sentence
that stage 1 keeps. Code lists every span of one to eight consecutive words of
the sentence. One Choice question asks which span is exactly the text the
rule says to quote. Up to three more rounds look for another problem in the
same sentence. Each names the quotes already chosen and offers "none". The
keep thresholds match the Method 1 runs: 0.5 for filler words, 0.45 for
missing actor.

| Pass | Method | F1 | P | R | Requests per draft | Cost per draft | Wall per draft |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| filler-words | Method 1 (rulenoul-nf) | 75.7%, 77.8% | 74%, 78% | 78%, 78% | 18, shared with nominalization | ~$0.0011 | ~5 s, shared |
| filler-words | Method 2 (jev-choice-fw) | 83.7%, 85.7% | 72%, 75% | 100%, 100% | 20 | $0.0022 | 6.2 s |
| passive-actor | Method 1 (rulenoul-pa) | 66.7%, 73.7% | 75%, 78% | 60%, 70% | 9 | $0.0011 | 2.0 s |
| passive-actor | Method 2 (jev-choice-pa) | 73.7%, 70.0% | 78%, 70% | 70%, 70% | 13 | $0.0015 | 4.4 s |
| filler-words | DeepSeek direct (ds-direct-hybrid) | 74.3%, 68.4% | 76%, 65% | 72%, 72% | - | - | - |
| passive-actor | DeepSeek direct (ds-direct-hybrid) | 60.0%, 57.1% | 60%, 55% | 60%, 60% | - | - | - |

Method 2 raises filler words by 8 F1 points and finds every reference item in
both runs. Missing actor moves by less than the spread between runs.

### 4c. Sentence openings with a clearer pronoun exclusion

`bench/rules/2026-09-23-jev-openings/` copies `2026-09-23-rewrite` and changes
`03-sentence-openings.md` only:

- A run by construction now means "the same introductory phrase or clause
  before the subject".
- The exclusion now reads: sentences that share only their first word, when
  that word is an article or a pronoun, are not a run. It gives an example
  that is not in the corpus ("He opened the door. He sat down. He waited.")
  and says that a subject followed by its verb is not a shared construction.

The code (`run-sentence-openings.ts`, Method 2) is unchanged, apart from a
`--rules` option.

Both pronoun-only runs are gone in both runs, and the real run ("The board…")

### 4d. The list-based method

`2026-09-23-jev.md` now starts with a note, and `bench/scripts/jev/README.md`
says the same. That earlier method uses hand-written English word lists and
patterns. It breaks the rule that a pass's rule text is its whole
definition, and it must not ship. The code is kept.
