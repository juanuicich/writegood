commit b5746ab. Its prompts equal `2026-09-23-jev-openings`. Three tests.

The current setup, as the author runs it:

## 1. Paragraph order on Gemini 3.8 Flash low, through the app's client

The app cannot pin an OpenRouter upstream. This test sends paragraph order
through the app's own client with OpenRouter as an `openai-compatible`
provider, as a user would configure it. The block is
`scripts/openrouter-app.toml`. `run.ts --provider app` sends each call
through `llm::chat` in the probe binary, so the request body, the thinking
options and the price are the app's.

What OpenRouter does for Gemini 3.8 Flash without pinning:

- Every endpoint that serves it is Google's own: Google AI Studio and Google
  Vertex, each at a standard, a flex and a priority tier. No third party
  serves it. So an unpinned request reaches the same vendor as a pinned one.
- Five small unpinned requests in the app's request shape all went to Google
  Vertex at the standard price, $0.75 and $3.75 per million tokens. The pinned
  runs went to Google AI Studio at the same price.
- The app sends `reasoning_effort` and DeepSeek's `thinking` switch.
  OpenRouter reads `reasoning_effort`: one question used 241 reasoning tokens
  at `low` and 6,255 at `high`.
- The app's client does not report which upstream served a call. The cost
  the app computed from its price catalog equals the credit OpenRouter
  charged: $0.00779 for the four drafts in one check.

The block needs `base_url = "https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/"`, with the slash
at the end. Without it, every call failed with `404 Not Found`. The likely
cause is URL joining: `chat/completions` joined onto `…/api/v1` replaces
`v1`. The DeepSeek block in `BENCHMARKS.md` has no slash and works, most
likely because DeepSeek also answers at `/chat/completions` without `/v1`.
This was not traced in the client's code.

An `openrouter` provider in the app works for paragraph order, once the base
URL ends with a slash. It scores the same as the pinned route, takes about 4
seconds a draft instead of 30 to 60 on DeepSeek at high, and costs about a
quarter of a cent a draft and under a cent a chapter.

## 2. Quality with windows

The four scored drafts hold 9,834 characters together, less than the 16,000
of one window. So `join.ts` joins them, in the order essay, memo, story,
on-writing, and adds paragraphs from the start of `chapter.md` until the
draft passes 18,000 characters. The result, `corpus/joined.md`, has 18,007
characters and 72 paragraphs. The padding has no reference. The scorer moves
each finding back to the draft it lies in and scores it against that draft's
reference. It drops findings in the padding.

The app splits `joined.md` into five windows:

| Window | Core paragraphs | Characters sent | Core holds |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1–14 | 5,659 | essay, start of memo |
| 2 | 15–27 | 5,783 | rest of memo, start of story |
| 3 | 28–51 | 6,622 | rest of story, on-writing, padding |
| 4 | 52–69 | 5,148 | padding |
| 5 | 70–72 | 1,140 | padding |

Two boundaries fall inside a scored draft, so some paragraphs of the memo
and the story are examined without the rest of their own draft.

Six passes ran, on DeepSeek direct, thinking off, with the verifier:
nominalization, missing actor, sentence openings, repeated phrasing, topic
flow and unearned metaphor. They hold 52 reference items.

- Paragraph order and length are left out. They judge the order and length
  of paragraphs in a whole document. In the joined draft, the four drafts
  and the padding are one document, so the reference items for each draft
  no longer describe it.
- Filler words is left out. In the current setup it runs on Jev, and Jev
  sees one paragraph and no window (SPEC §8.4), so windows cannot change its
  answers.

`--single-window 20000` sends the whole joined draft in every call, as the
app does for a draft under 16,000 characters. The third column is the same
six passes on the four drafts sent separately, from the DeepSeek part of test
3 (`noise-ds-1` to `-4`, scored with `--skip paragraph-order,length`).

- Windows do not lower quality that this test can see. The means are 68.9%
  with windows and 68.0% with one window. The two runs of each differ by 6
  and 12 points, more than the gap between the two means.
- Repeated phrasing (7 to 11 points) and nominalization (1 to 10 points)
  score higher with windows in both runs. Each difference is one or two
  findings. The other passes move in both directions.
- At 18,000 characters, windows cost a little more, not less. Each window
  has its own verification, which adds about 60 calls, and its own prompt prefix,
  which the cache must fill again. The saving in input tokens grows with the
  draft's length. On the 5,000-word chapter the app measured it.
- Each configuration ran twice, on 52 items. A loss of 5 points or less
  would not show.

## 3. Run-to-run noise of the current setup

Four runs of the current setup on the four scored drafts. Each run is
composed with `merge.ts`: the DeepSeek part (`noise-ds-N`: eight passes,
paragraph order at high) and the Jev part (`noise-jev-N`: filler words, as
the app runs it through `jev.ts` and `jev::ask`).

| Pass | Items | noise-1, -2, -3, -4 | Mean | SD | Range |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| filler-words (Jev) | 18 | 81.8, 81.8, 81.8, 71.8 | 79.3% | 5.0 | 71.8–81.8 |
| length | 6 | 66.7, 60.0, 50.0, 40.0 | 54.2% | 11.7 | 40.0–66.7 |
| nominalization | 14 | 92.3, 92.3, 84.6, 84.6 | 88.5% | 4.4 | 84.6–92.3 |
| paragraph-order | 6 | 76.9, 75.0, 83.3, 85.7 | 80.2% | 5.1 | 75.0–85.7 |
| passive-actor | 10 | 50.0, 63.6, 58.8, 52.6 | 56.3% | 6.1 | 50.0–63.6 |
| repeated-phrasing | 11 | 66.7, 61.5, 60.9, 71.4 | 65.1% | 4.9 | 60.9–71.4 |
| sentence-openings | 5 | 72.7, 80.0, 44.4, 88.9 | 71.5% | 19.2 | 44.4–88.9 |
| topic-flow | 5 | 23.1, 33.3, 19.0, 44.4 | 30.0% | 11.4 | 19.0–44.4 |
| unearned-metaphor | 7 | 82.4, 92.3, 76.9, 85.7 | 84.3% | 6.4 | 76.9–92.3 |
| **All passes, F1** | 82 | 68.9, 72.4, 65.5, 71.4 | **69.6%** | **3.1** | 65.5–72.4 |
| All passes, precision | | 62.4, 65.0, 61.1, 69.8 | 64.6% | 3.8 | 61.1–69.8 |
| All passes, recall | | 76.8, 81.7, 70.7, 73.2 | 75.6% | 4.8 | 70.7–81.7 |

SD is the sample standard deviation over the four runs, in F1 points.

- First findings arrived in 4.3 to 5.5 seconds per draft. The DeepSeek part
  took 30 to 59 seconds per draft, set by paragraph order at high.
- Cost per draft was $0.022 to $0.026: $0.019 to $0.024 for DeepSeek, $0.0023
  for Jev.
- Jev scored 81.8% in three runs with the same 18 hits and 8 false findings.
  In run 4, one request returned `503 Service Unavailable`, and the benchmark
  lost that paragraph's findings. That cost 4 hits. In the app, the failed
  request would stop the pass, so it would keep only the paragraphs already
  answered.
- Jev's wall times here, 27 to 71 seconds per draft, are the harness's. It
  starts a probe process per request, and four drafts share 8 requests in
  flight. They do not measure the app.
- DeepSeek returned one unreadable reply in runs 2 and 4, and none in runs 1
  and 3.
- Topic flow scores 30% because DeepSeek flags many false topic-flow
  findings: 18, 10, 14 and 2 in the four runs, against 5 reference items.

What the numbers mean for a later change:

- One run of the whole setup lands within about 4 points of 69.6%. Two single
  runs can differ by 7 points (65.5 against 72.4) with nothing changed.
- To compare a change with this baseline, run it four times and compare the
  means. The standard error of a four-run mean is about 1.6 points, so a
  difference below about 4.5 points between two four-run means is noise.
- Per pass, the noise is larger. A pass with 5 or 6 reference items (sentence
  openings, topic flow, paragraph order, length) moves 11 to 45 points
  between runs of the same setup. Judge a rule change for one of those passes
  on more drafts, not on these four.
