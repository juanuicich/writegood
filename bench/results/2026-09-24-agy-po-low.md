# Paragraph order on agy at thinking low

24 September 2026. Rule set `2026-09-23-british`, which matches
`src-tauri/passes`. Paragraph order alone, pipeline `plain`, on agy with
`--thinking low`. The block's `thinking_names` maps `low` to
`gemini-3.8-flash-low`. Each call went through the app's runner
(`src-tauri/examples/cli.rs`) with `scripts/agy.toml`. The plan charges
nothing per call.

```
bun bench/scripts/run.ts --provider agy --model gemini-3.8-flash --thinking low \
  --pipeline plain --passes paragraph-order --rules 2026-09-23-british --label agy-po-low-1
```

Paragraph order was measured on agy only at thinking high before this test.
There a call took 78 to 125 seconds of service time, 103 to 181 seconds in
all, and about 40,000 thinking tokens.

On the chapter (`agy-po-low-chapter`, 5,038 words, 8,589 input tokens), the
call took 9.7 seconds, with 292 output tokens. It returned three findings.
The app had taken 110 to 145 seconds for this pass on DeepSeek at high
(`app-runs.md`), and 4.1 seconds on Gemini low through OpenRouter.

- agy at low scored 80.0% and 85.7%. Both runs found all six reference
  items. They flagged three and two other paragraphs. The Gemini-low runs
  through OpenRouter scored 80.0% twice, and the DeepSeek-high runs 75.0% to
  92.3%. On six items, one finding moves the score by 5 to 15 points, so these
  scores are the same within noise.
- agy at high scored 92.3% in both runs, one false finding fewer than low's
  better run. Those runs used the `rewrite` rules, not the British ones.
  The two paragraph-order rules differ only in quotation marks.
- agy at low reports no thinking tokens. At high it used about 40,000 a call.
- In run 1, each call waited 5.8 seconds while the harness built the example
  binary with cargo, once per process. Service time, 6.1 to 6.3 seconds, is
  the fair figure. The app has no such wait.
- In run 2, three calls took 4.9 to 6.6 seconds. The essay call took 20.3
  seconds and returned 5,524 output tokens: a JSON array, then its own
  check of that array in plain text, then a second array. The parser read
  the reply, and the call counts as a success.
- agy's start-up adds about two seconds to each call (SPEC §9.3). This test
  did not measure it apart from the call. With it taken off, agy answers in
  about 3 to 4 seconds, as the API does.
- agy sends more input tokens than the API, about 2,200 against 1,250 on a
  draft. The difference is agy's own prompt.
- No call failed. agy reported no quota or rate-limit error in the nine calls.
  Four calls ran at once, below the limit of 8 that `agy.toml` sets.

## Conclusion

Paragraph order on agy at thinking low is a free, fast option. It scored
80% and 86%, as Gemini low through OpenRouter and DeepSeek at high did. It
took about 6 seconds a draft and 10 seconds on the chapter, against 30 to 60
seconds on DeepSeek at high and 80 to 180 seconds on agy at high. It costs
nothing on a Google AI plan. One call in eight took 20 seconds.

The frontmatter of `06-paragraph-order.md` that uses it, with the agy block
from SPEC §9.3 in `config.toml`:

```toml
+++
name = "Paragraph order"
category = "paragraph-order"
scope = "document"
enabled = true
provider = "agy"
thinking = "low"

+++
```
